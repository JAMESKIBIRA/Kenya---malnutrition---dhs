## 1. Introduction

Child Malnutrition remains a critical public health in Kenya. It affects child survival, cognitive development, and long-term national productivity. According to the 2022 Kenya Demographic and Health Survey (KDHS), a significant portion of children under five are affected by stunting, wasting, and underweight. This project uses the KDHS 2022 dataset to analyze the national prevalence of malnutrition and identify the key socioeconomic determinants, providing evidence for targeted interventions

## 2. Methodology

Data was obtained from the Kenya DHS 2022 Children's Recode (KR) file, comprising 19,530 children and 1,312 variables.
- **Data Cleaning:** Missing values (coded as 9998) were excluded.
- **Indicators:** Z-scores were used to create binary indicators for stunting (height-for-age < -2 SD), wasting (weight-for-height < -2 SD), and underweight (weight-for-age < -2 SD).
- **Weighting:** DHS survey weights (`v005`) were applied to ensure nationally representative estimates.
- **Analysis:** Data analysis was conducted in R using the `haven` and `tidyverse` packages. A multivariable logistic regression model was used to assess the association between malnutrition and socioeconomic factors (maternal education, wealth index, and place of residence).

## 3. Results 
### 3.1 National Prevalence

The weighted national prevalence of malnutrition among children under five in Kenya is:
- **Stunting:** 17.35%
- **Wasting:** 10.03%
- **Underweight:** 4.89%

### 3.2 Geographic Disparities

There are significant regional disparities. The top 5 counties with the highest stunting prevalence are:
1. Kilifi (36.9%)
2. West Pokot (33.8%)
3. Samburu (30.2%)
4. Meru (25.7%)
5. Bomet (23.1%)


*(See the `output/stunting_top_10_counties.png` chart for a visual representation).*

### 3.3 Determinants of Stunting (Regression Analysis)

The logistic regression model revealed that three factors are statistically significant in reducing the risk of stunting:
1. **Maternal Education:** Higher education levels significantly decrease the risk (p < 0.001).
2. **Wealth Index:** Higher household wealth significantly decreases the risk (p < 0.001).
3. **Residence:** Living in an urban area significantly reduces the risk compared to rural areas (p < 0.05).

## 4. Conclusion 

While 17.35% stunting is a national average, certain counties (like Kilifi and West Pokot) are experiencing a crisis. The data clearly shows that improving maternal education and household wealth are the most effective ways to combat malnutrition.
